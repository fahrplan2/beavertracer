# Subnetting

:::goal
**Learning goal:** You can explain why networks are divided, divide a network into equal-sized subnets and implement the subnets in a network with a router.
:::

:::sim
url=/sims/lesson-4.4.2.btsim
:::

A school is given the address range `192.168.10.0/24` for its network — enough for 254 devices. You *could* put all devices into this one network. But usually it's better to divide it into several smaller networks. This process is called **subnetting**, the smaller networks **subnets**.

Why is it worth it?

- **Security:** the administration, teachers and students are in separate networks. Between the networks there's a router — there you can define exactly who may go where.
- **Fewer broadcasts:** every broadcast (e.g. every ARP request) reaches *all* devices in its own network. Smaller networks mean less unnecessary traffic.
- **Clarity:** from the address you can immediately tell where a device belongs.

:::note
Remember the VLANs from chapter 3.2? VLANs separate networks on **layer 2**, subnetting separates them on **layer 3**. In practice the two usually go together: every VLAN gets its own subnet.
:::
