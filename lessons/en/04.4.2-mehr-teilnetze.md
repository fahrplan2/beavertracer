# More subnets, other octets

Colours as in the previous section: [[n|network part]] · [[e|extension]] · [[h|host part]]

## Subnets or hosts — you have to decide

Every additional extension bit doubles the number of subnets — and halves the size of each subnet. For a `/24` network it looks like this:

| Subnets | Prefix length | Netmask | Addresses per subnet | Hosts per subnet |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | `/25` | `255.255.255.128` | 128 | 126 |
| 4 | `/26` | `255.255.255.192` | 64 | 62 |
| 8 | `/27` | `255.255.255.224` | 32 | 30 |
| 16 | `/28` | `255.255.255.240` | 16 | 14 |
| 32 | `/29` | `255.255.255.248` | 8 | 6 |
| 64 | `/30` | `255.255.255.252` | 4 | 2 |

So when planning you have to check both: are there **enough subnets** — and are they **big enough** for all devices?

## The shortcut: the block size

Once you've understood the method in binary, it can be done faster. The number of addresses per subnet is called the **block size**. It follows from the number of host bits in the octet where the boundary lies — or simply as **256 minus the mask octet**:

- `/26` → mask `255.255.255.192` → 256 − 192 = **64** → subnets at .0, .64, .128, .192
- `/27` → mask `255.255.255.224` → 256 − 224 = **32** → subnets at .0, .32, .64, .96 …

## Example: eight subnets

`192.168.42.0/24` into **eight** subnets: 3 bits (2³ = 8), `/24` becomes `/27`, mask `255.255.255.224`, block size 32.

| Subnet | EEE | Network address | Broadcast address | Range for hosts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [[e|000]] | `192.168.42.0` | `192.168.42.31` | `.1` – `.30` |
| 2 | [[e|001]] | `192.168.42.32` | `192.168.42.63` | `.33` – `.62` |
| 3 | [[e|010]] | `192.168.42.64` | `192.168.42.95` | `.65` – `.94` |
| 4 | [[e|011]] | `192.168.42.96` | `192.168.42.127` | `.97` – `.126` |
| 5 | [[e|100]] | `192.168.42.128` | `192.168.42.159` | `.129` – `.158` |
| 6 | [[e|101]] | `192.168.42.160` | `192.168.42.191` | `.161` – `.190` |
| 7 | [[e|110]] | `192.168.42.192` | `192.168.42.223` | `.193` – `.222` |
| 8 | [[e|111]] | `192.168.42.224` | `192.168.42.255` | `.225` – `.254` |

## When the boundary is in a different octet

The method works the same way when the original network is larger than `/24`. Then the extension simply lies in a different octet — and **all octets after it** belong entirely to the host part.

### Example: `172.20.0.0/16` into four subnets

2 extension bits, `/16` becomes `/18` (mask `255.255.192.0`). The extension is now at the start of the **third** octet, the fourth octet is entirely host part:

<pre class="bits-block">
[[n|172.20.]] [[e|E E]] [[h|H H H H H H . H H H H H H H H]]
</pre>

| Subnet | EE | Network address (host bits 0) | Broadcast address (host bits 1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [[e|00]] | `172.20.0.0` | `172.20.63.255` |
| 2 | [[e|01]] | `172.20.64.0` | `172.20.127.255` |
| 3 | [[e|10]] | `172.20.128.0` | `172.20.191.255` |
| 4 | [[e|11]] | `172.20.192.0` | `172.20.255.255` |

Careful: the broadcast address of subnet 1 is `172.20.63.255` — not `172.20.63.0`. In the fourth octet all bits are host bits, after all, and they're set to 1 for the broadcast address. Every subnet has 2¹⁴ − 2 = 16,382 hosts.

### Example: `10.16.0.0/12` into eight subnets

3 extension bits, `/12` becomes `/15` (mask `255.254.0.0`). This time the boundary is in the **second** octet. The number 16 is `0001 0000` in binary; the first four bits already belong to the network part, then come the three extension bits and one host bit:

<pre class="bits-block">
[[n|10. 0 0 0 1]] [[e|E E E]] [[h|H . H H H H H H H H . H H H H H H H H]]
</pre>

| Subnet | EEE | second octet (network address) | Network address | Broadcast address |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [[e|000]] | [[n|0001]] [[e|000]] [[h|0]] = 16 | `10.16.0.0` | `10.17.255.255` |
| 2 | [[e|001]] | [[n|0001]] [[e|001]] [[h|0]] = 18 | `10.18.0.0` | `10.19.255.255` |
| 3 | [[e|010]] | [[n|0001]] [[e|010]] [[h|0]] = 20 | `10.20.0.0` | `10.21.255.255` |
| 4 | [[e|011]] | [[n|0001]] [[e|011]] [[h|0]] = 22 | `10.22.0.0` | `10.23.255.255` |
| 5 | [[e|100]] | [[n|0001]] [[e|100]] [[h|0]] = 24 | `10.24.0.0` | `10.25.255.255` |
| 6 | [[e|101]] | [[n|0001]] [[e|101]] [[h|0]] = 26 | `10.26.0.0` | `10.27.255.255` |
| 7 | [[e|110]] | [[n|0001]] [[e|110]] [[h|0]] = 28 | `10.28.0.0` | `10.29.255.255` |
| 8 | [[e|111]] | [[n|0001]] [[e|111]] [[h|0]] = 30 | `10.30.0.0` | `10.31.255.255` |

:::warning Typical mistakes
- **Prefix increased wrongly:** for 4 subnets you add 2 bits, not 4 — it's the number of bits that counts, not the number of networks.
- **Wrong octet:** the extension follows directly on from the previous network part. With a `/16` it's in the third octet, not the fourth.
- **Forgetting the later octets:** if the boundary isn't in the last octet, all following octets are host part — so `255` for the broadcast address.
- **Assigning the network or broadcast address to devices:** the host range starts one address *after* the network address and ends one *before* the broadcast address.
:::
