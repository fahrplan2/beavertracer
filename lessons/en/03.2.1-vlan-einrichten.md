# One switch, two networks

:::sim
url=/sims/lesson-3.2.1.btsim
:::

Four PCs are connected to the switch: the **Sekretariat** (school office, port 1) and the **Schulleitung** (head teacher, port 2) from the administration, plus **Anna** (port 3) and **Ben** (port 4) from the computer room. All of them are in the same network `192.168.0.0/24`.

## The problem

Switch to :fa-play: **Run** mode, open the :fa-terminal: **Terminal** on **Anna** and ping the school office:

```
$ ping 192.168.0.10
```

Anna reaches the school office without any trouble. That's exactly what we want to prevent.

## Setting up VLANs

A **VLAN** (*Virtual LAN*) is a separate, partitioned-off network inside a switch. Every VLAN has a number, the **VLAN ID**. We decide: the administration goes into **VLAN 10**, the computer room into **VLAN 20**.

1. In Run mode, click the **Switch** and open the **VLAN** tab.
2. Tick **enable VLAN**. A table appears with one row per port.
3. In the **PVID** column, enter the VLAN ID — `10` for **port 1** and **port 2**, `20` for **port 3** and **port 4**. Leave the **Mode** at `untagged`.
4. Click **Apply** at the bottom.

:::note
**PVID** stands for *Port VLAN ID*: everything that arrives at this port belongs to this VLAN. The mode `untagged` means that a completely normal PC is connected to this port, which doesn't need to know anything about VLANs.
:::

## Testing

Ping the school office from **Anna** once more — and then Ben (`ping 192.168.0.21`).

:::quiz mc
What happens now?
- [ ] Anna reaches the school office and Ben
- [x] Anna reaches Ben, but not the school office any more
- [ ] Anna can't reach anyone any more
:::

:::evaluate
Check answer
:::

In :fa-magnifying-glass: **Trace** mode, open a trace for the **Sekretariat**: Anna's ARP requests ("Who has 192.168.0.10?") don't show up there at all. Her broadcast stays in VLAN 20 — for the switch these are now two separate networks that don't notice anything of each other.

:::tip Key point
With **VLANs** you divide a switch into several logically separated networks. Devices in different VLANs can't reach each other directly — even broadcasts stay in their own VLAN. Every VLAN has a number, the **VLAN ID**.
:::
