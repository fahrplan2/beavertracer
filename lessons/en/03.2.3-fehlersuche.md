# Troubleshooting VLANs

:::sim
url=/sims/lesson-3.2.3.btsim
:::

After some rebuilding over the weekend, the school reports on Monday: "**Ben** can suddenly get to the administration computers — but not to Anna any more." Find the faults and fix them.

## The network plan

This is how the network is *supposed* to be set up:

| Device | Switch | Port | VLAN | IP address |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sekretariat | Switch EG | 1 | 10 | `192.168.0.10` |
| Anna | Switch EG | 2 | 20 | `192.168.0.20` |
| Schulleitung | Switch OG | 1 | 10 | `192.168.0.11` |
| Ben | Switch OG | 2 | 20 | `192.168.0.21` |

Port 8 is the trunk on both switches: mode `tagged`, allowed VLANs `10,20`.

<details>
<summary>Tip 1: Where do I start?</summary>

Open the **VLAN** tab on both switches and compare every row with the network plan: the PVID of the PC ports and the allowed VLANs on the trunk.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Tip 2: There are two faults</summary>

One fault explains why Ben can get to the administration. The other explains why he can't reach Anna — both are on **Switch OG**.
</details>

## What was going on?

:::quiz fill
Ben was in VLAN {10} instead of VLAN {20}. In addition, the trunk on switch {OG|upper floor} didn't let VLAN {20} through.
:::

:::evaluate
Check answers
:::

:::tip Key point
Typical VLAN faults: a port with the **wrong PVID** (the device ends up in the wrong VLAN) and a **trunk that doesn't allow a VLAN** (devices in the same VLAN on different switches can't reach each other). So always check *both* ends of a trunk.
:::
