Now there are two tablets in the Wi-Fi `Schule`: **Anna** (`192.168.0.30`) and **Ben** (`192.168.0.31`).

Remember chapter 3.1: when Anna pinged Ben through the switch, Clara didn't notice anything — the switch sent the pings only to Ben's port. Is it the same in the Wi-Fi? Think briefly about what you expect.

2. Switch to :fa-magnifying-glass: **Trace** mode and use **+** to open a trace for **Ben**, port `wlan0`.

## What's behind it

Radio waves spread out in all directions. So whatever Anna sends reaches *every* device nearby — not just the access point. Radio is a **shared medium**. Ben's tablet normally ignores everything that isn't addressed to it. But anyone who *wants* to record can read everything.

| | Switch (cable) | Wi-Fi (radio) |
|---|---|---|
| Who gets a unicast? | only the receiver's port | every device in range |
| Can a third party listen in? | normally not | yes, easily |

:::tip Key point
Radio is a **shared medium**: everything sent in a Wi-Fi network can be received by every device in range. That's why a Wi-Fi network has to be **encrypted** (today with **WPA2** or **WPA3**) — then strangers can still receive the data, but not read it.
:::

:::note
BeaverTracer doesn't simulate Wi-Fi encryption — that's why you can read everything here. How encryption works you'll learn in chapter 8.
:::
