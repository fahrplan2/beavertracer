# A connection from start to finish

:::sim
url=/sims/lesson-5.1.btsim
:::

The echo service runs on port `5000` on the **Server**: it immediately sends every message back. With the **Simple TCP Client** you can control a connection step by step — every button is one phase.

## The experiment

1. Switch to :fa-play: **Run** mode, click the **Client-PC** and open the **Simple TCP Client**.
2. Enter `192.168.0.20` under **Host** and `5000` under **Port**.
3. Click **Connect**.
4. Type `Hello World` into the message field and click **Send**. Right after that you see the server's echo in the chat window.
5. Click **Disconnect**.
6. Switch to :fa-magnifying-glass: **Trace** mode and use **+** to open a trace for the **Client-PC**, port `eth0`.

:::note
You already know the two ARP packets at the start from chapter 1. In the **Info** column of the TCP packets there's first `49152 → 5000` (from which port to which port), then the **flags** in square brackets and after that numbers like `Seq=`, `Ack=` and `Len=`. The numbers are covered from page 5.2.3 on — here you look at the flags and `Len`.
:::

## Phase 2: Send

`Len` states how many bytes of payload a packet carries.

:::quiz short
How many bytes does the packet with your message carry (`Len`)?
= 13
:::

:::note
`Hello World` only has 11 characters. But the TCP client appends a line ending (2 bytes, `\r\n`) — that's what many internet protocols like SMTP or HTTP expect.
:::

:::quiz mc
What does the server send back directly after your message — even before the echo?
- [x] A packet with `[ACK]` and `Len=0` — an acknowledgement without data
- [ ] Nothing at all
- [ ] A new `[SYN]`
:::
