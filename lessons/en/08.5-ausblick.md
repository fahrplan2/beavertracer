# Summary and Outlook

## The building blocks at a glance

| Building block | Security goal | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Symmetric encryption** | Confidentiality | AES encrypts the data of an HTTPS connection |
| **Asymmetric encryption** | Confidentiality, key exchange | Diffie-Hellman, RSA |
| **Hashes** | Integrity | SHA-256 checksum of a download |
| **Digital signature** | Authenticity, integrity | the server signs its Diffie-Hellman share |
| **Certificate** | Authenticity | confirms: this key belongs to `www.biberbank.de` |

## The same principle in many places

What you've learned about HTTPS is also inside many other technologies:

- **Wi-Fi (WPA2/WPA3):** in chapter 3.3.2, every device in range could listen in. With WPA2 or WPA3 every radio frame is encrypted with AES; WPA3 also negotiates the key in such a way that an eavesdropper can't decrypt old recordings even with the Wi-Fi password.
- **SSH:** the encrypted remote administration from chapter 6.6. Instead of certificates, the client remembers the **fingerprint** of the server's key on the first connection and warns if it changes later.
- **VPN:** an encrypted tunnel through the internet, e.g. into the school or company network. That's the topic of chapter 10.
- **Messengers:** Signal, WhatsApp and others encrypt **end-to-end**: not even the operator of the service can read the messages.

:::note
Large **quantum computers** could one day crack the splitting of large numbers into prime factors — and with it RSA and Diffie-Hellman. That's why new, **quantum-safe** methods are already being introduced today. Symmetric ciphers like AES with long keys are still considered secure.
:::
