# The most important address types

With IPv6 you can tell from the start of an address what it's meant for:

| Address type | recognisable by | what for |
|---|---|---|
| **Link-local** | `fe80::` | Only valid in your **own local network**. Every device gives itself this address automatically — it's never forwarded by a router. |
| **Global unicast** | `2…` or `3…` (`2000::/3`) | Globally unique address, reachable on the internet. The prefix comes from the internet provider. |
| **Unique local** | `fd…` (`fc00::/7`) | For private networks, comparable to `192.168.x.x` with IPv4. |
| **Loopback** | `::1` | Your own device — like `127.0.0.1` with IPv4. |
| **Multicast** | `ff…` | To a **group** of devices, e.g. `ff02::1` = all devices in the local network. |

:::note
The range `2001:db8::/32` is reserved for examples and teaching material — it never occurs on the real internet. That's why you see it in this course.
:::

An important new feature: **IPv6 has no broadcast any more.** What was a broadcast to everyone with IPv4, IPv6 handles with **multicast** addresses — the message only goes to the devices that are interested in that group.

:::tip Key point
Every IPv6 device has at least one **link-local address** (`fe80::…`) for its own network, usually also a **global address** for the internet. Instead of broadcast, IPv6 uses **multicast** (`ff…`).
:::
