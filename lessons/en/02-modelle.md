# Models

:::goal
**Learning goal:** You can explain how a packet is built up in layers (encapsulation), name the layers in the tracer, and explain and compare the ISO/OSI and the TCP/IP reference model.
:::

In chapter 1 you saw that a single click on a link involves a whole range of different protocols: ARP, TCP, HTTP — each with its own job. To sort this multitude of protocols sensibly, network engineering works with layer models.

In this chapter you first take a single packet apart and then get to know the two most important models: the ISO/OSI reference model with its seven layers and the somewhat more pragmatic TCP/IP model with four. You'll meet both again and again in the following chapters — they are the shared vocabulary we use to talk about networks from now on.
